From a conversation to useful information

ASISVIA explores virtual agents and voice interaction for collecting and analysing patient experience. The project is led by Hospitales Universitarios San Roque in collaboration with research partners including UPV / LabLENI.

My contribution was the conception of a generative AI system for managing interviews and analysing their results. It brings together my work on agent-based systems and my research background in human behaviour.

Official project description ↗

The concept I worked on

The important connection is between what an interview asks and what its analysis can legitimately conclude. Both need to serve the same information goal.

The architectural questions

Interaction

Where should the conversation adapt?

A generative interview can follow up on a response. The design question is how to preserve the interview’s purpose while allowing that flexibility: which topics must be covered, what can vary and when the interaction should end.

Analysis

How does a result stay connected to its source?

The analysis should distinguish what a participant said from the interpretation of that response. A useful design review asks which interview evidence supports a result, and what uncertainty needs to remain visible.

These questions explain the system-level reasoning behind the concept. Specific session-management mechanisms, model providers and deployment choices are outside the implementation evidence presented here.

Contribution and project scope

The public project description covers satisfaction analysis, emotional-state research, virtual reality and user management. My account here focuses on the generative interview and results-analysis concept.

This case presents conceptual work and high-level design. It does not claim that I implemented every module, delivered a production deployment or validated clinical outcomes.

What this case demonstrates: framing a generative AI application around an interaction and its downstream information needs, within a multidisciplinary research project.
